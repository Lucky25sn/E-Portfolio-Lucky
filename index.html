1. Agile Team Charter

The Agile Team Charter defined team roles, communication methods, working agreements, project goals, ceremonies, and the Definition of Done for the LaunchPad.dev project.

Evidence: Agile Team Charter Document

Reflection

Creating the Agile Team Charter strengthened my understanding of Agile principles, teamwork, communication, accountability, and project planning. As the Technical and Feasibility Lead, I ensured that the proposed solution was practical and aligned with project requirements.

2. User Stories and Tasks

User stories helped the team focus on delivering value to users while breaking large requirements into manageable development tasks.

Evidence: User Stories and Tasks Document

Reflection

Through planning user stories, I improved my understanding of Agile requirements gathering, backlog organisation, API planning, integration, accessibility, testing, and deployment activities.

3. Product Backlog

The Product Backlog organised project requirements, priorities, sprint objectives, and ownership responsibilities throughout the LaunchPad.dev project lifecycle.

Reflection

Maintaining the backlog improved project visibility, accountability, sprint planning, prioritisation, and collaboration across the team.

4. Sprint Backlog

Sprint Backlogs were maintained weekly to ensure project work was completed incrementally and aligned to Agile delivery principles.

Reflection

Working through sprint cycles taught me the importance of planning, communication, retrospectives, and continuous improvement.

5. Agile Logbook

The Agile Logbook recorded daily stand-ups, tasks completed, planned activities, blockers, and lessons learned throughout the project.

Reflection

Maintaining the logbook improved my organisation, self-management, accountability, and ability to communicate progress effectively.
